# Audience strategy from the outreach lead files

> Analyzed 2026-09-05 from 35 lead-tool exports (12,794 companies, 16,398 contacts,
> deduplicated). Aggregates only. No name, email or profile URL is recorded here or
> anywhere in the repository. The reader section of `site-brief.md` carries the same
> figures; this file carries the consequences.

## The finding in one paragraph

The people Camzify is about to email are owners of small security businesses, not
corporate security managers. 56% run guarding companies (NACE 80.1), 34% install CCTV,
alarms and access control (NACE 80.2), 87% have 1–50 staff, and 71% of the contacts
who carry a title are the owner, CEO, managing director or Geschäftsführer. 35% are in
the US, 30% in Germany, 14% in the UK. Of their own service descriptions, guarding
companies mention mobile patrols (15%), manned guarding (9%), CCTV (8%) and alarm
monitoring (4%); installers mention CCTV (53%), access control (31%), fire (17%) and
monitoring (17%). **0% mention remote or virtual guarding.** The website was written for
the end customer with a partner track on the side. The lead list says the partner track
is the front door.

## Who is reading

- **The guarding company owner.** 10–40 guards across a dozen client sites. Sells guard
  hours and mobile patrols, loses contracts on price, cannot staff overnight rounds, has
  never seen a compliance report with photographs. Reads on a phone. Wants a price
  before booking anything.
- **The CCTV and alarm installer.** 3–10 people. Installs and moves to the next job. No
  recurring revenue beyond maintenance. Knows every camera brand. Would sell a monthly
  service to existing customers if it needed no new hardware and no new staff.

Both are decision-maker and buyer in one person. The argument that wins is revenue and
margin, not cost-cutting. A page that tells a guarding company owner what their client
saves by not hiring guards is telling them their business is the problem.

## Eight consequences for the site

1. **The partner track becomes the primary track.** Top-level "Solutions for" nav
   (guarding companies, installers, monitoring companies, then end customers) and a
   segment door in the homepage hero band, not a paragraph in the cost section.
2. **Vocabulary shifts to the buyer's.** "Installer", "alarm", "mobile patrol",
   "keyholding" on every partner page and every path into them. Technical pages keep
   their terms.
3. **Introduce the category against what they sell now.** Comparison pages against mobile
   patrols and against alarm monitoring, written for the company that sells those.
4. **The argument is revenue.** "Sell overnight coverage you cannot staff" and "install
   once, bill monthly". An agency-side revenue calculator beside the end-customer
   savings calculator.
5. **Pricing has to say something.** Publish what drives a quote and the entry floor the
   business has stated internally, or accept that ten-person companies will not book a
   demo to learn a price. Business decision.
6. **Germany is a third of the list.** A six-to-eight-page German pilot of the partner
   track with hreflang is the cheapest test of whether that 30% converts.
7. **Outreach needs landing pages.** One short page per segment, one CTA, the owner's
   language, UTM-ready, linked from the cold email instead of the homepage.
8. **Alarm verification is a missing use case.** Verifying an alarm with the camera
   before dispatch is the first thing an alarm-monitoring company will recognize.

## Roadmap

**Phase 1 (no decisions needed):** "Solutions for" nav; homepage segment door; retitle
the integrator page for CCTV and alarm installers (URL unchanged); three outreach
landing pages; two comparison pages (vs mobile patrols, adding remote patrols to alarm
monitoring); alarm verification use case; owner guides (how to sell virtual patrolling
to a client, what a client proposal contains, a sample client report); agency revenue
calculator.

**Phase 2 (decisions):** pricing disclosure; German pilot with euro pricing if any
pricing is shown; country pages for US, UK and Germany naming local terms (ARC, NSL,
Werkschutz) and the compliance posture honestly.

**Phase 3 (after first conversions):** further languages only if German moves; case
studies only when a partner agrees to be named; a partner area only if partners ask.

## Decisions for the business

1. Pricing disclosure: entry floor and quote drivers, or fully quote-based.
2. German: fund the pilot, or measure the drop on English pages first.
3. Partner terms: state a standard margin or tier if one exists; otherwise the pages stay
   as they are.
4. Homepage lead: partner buyer first, end customer second, is what the list supports.
5. Outreach compliance: 60% of contacts are in the EU; the privacy page should state the
   legitimate-interest basis for B2B outreach before the first email. Legal check, not
   copy.

## Method and caveats

Segment is the best-scoring NACE activity per company. Country comes from company
records because 90% of contact records carry no location. Service signals are keyword
matches on companies' own descriptions and undercount. 60% of contacts carry no job
title; the 71% owner figure is of those who do.
